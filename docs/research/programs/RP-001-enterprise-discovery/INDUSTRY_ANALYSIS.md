## 1. Thesis

Enterprises fail AI and operating-model programs less often from “insufficient models” and more often from **undiscovered enterprise truth**: capability confused with organization, readiness reduced to licenses, knowledge without authority, and stage labels without evidence. Industry patterns below motivate EDF’s eleven domains and Cap≠Org rule.

## 3. Sector Stress Notes (T1)

### 3.1 Discrete / Hybrid Manufacturing

- Quality and safety regimes raise **Compliance Reflex**; discovery must not invent bypasses.  
- Changeover **Exception Density** falsifies naive automation affinity.  
- OT knowledge stickiness caps AI workforce scale even when MES exists (WT-01).  
- Cap≠Org fails when “Quality Dept” is treated as the Quality Containment capability.

### 3.2 Professional / Knowledge Services

- Client-data and IP regimes dominate Risk Posture; assistive drafting still needs residual humans.  
- Commercial urgency amplifies license theater (WT-02).  
- Capability “Delivery Governance” ≠ practice org boxes.  
- Partner Embeddedness DNA axis shapes Marketplace/package appetite later (research only).

### 3.3 Distribution / Hybrid Growth Enterprises

- Stage contrast (WT-03) shows **Stage ≠ Potential**: S5 integration history can raise Potential while knowledge authority still caps agentic inventory commits.  
- Tool sprawl at S4/S5 creates false “AI-ready” narratives without IdP/approval design.

## 5. Implications for EDF (Research)

1. Keep **eleven domains** — collapsing Knowledge into Infrastructure recreates P4/P8.  
2. Keep **mandatory Cap≠Org sessions** — P2 is ubiquitous.  
3. Keep **AI Readiness multi-pillar** — P1/P3 are dominant commercial narratives.  
4. Keep **stage criterion hits** — P6 is a governance risk, not cosmetic.  
5. Keep **advisory-only dossier exports** to RP-005/RP-007 — P7.
