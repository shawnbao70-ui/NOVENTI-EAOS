## 1. Research Objective

Produce a reusable Enterprise Discovery Framework covering Profile, DNA, Capability, Organization, AI/Automation/Infrastructure readiness, Knowledge, Growth Stage, Evolution Potential, and AI Roadmap.

## 2. Business Background

EAOS Foundation is architecturally mature. Enterprises cannot be transformed by installing a platform alone. Without disciplined discovery, AI roadmaps become tool shopping and org charts are mistaken for capabilities.

## 3. Industry Problems

1. Transformation starts from vendor features, not enterprise truth.  
2. Org charts treated as operating models.  
3. AI readiness reduced to model/API access.  
4. Knowledge authority unclear.  
5. Automation ignores exception density.  
6. Growth labels are vanity maturity theater.  
7. No durable link from discovery to governed evolution advice.

## 4. Future Trends

| Trend | Confidence | Horizon |
|-------|------------|---------|
| Continuous enterprise sensing | High | 2–4 years |
| Capability graphs over org charts | High | 2–5 years |
| Multi-dimensional AI readiness | High | Now–3 years |
| Evolution engines consume live discovery | Medium | 3–6 years |

## 5. Enterprise Value

Safer AI investment; fewer failed automations; shared language across business/IT/OT; evidence for Evolution Engine; repeatable EAOS adoption.

## 6. Capability Model

Discovery itself is a meta-capability: **Sense → Structure → Score → Dossier → Advise**.  
Outputs: Profile, DNA vector, Capability Graph, Org Map, Knowledge Map, Readiness bands, Stage, Evolution Potential, AI Roadmap.  
Maturity constructs: Growth Stage S1–S6; AI Readiness bands; Evolution Potential band (research constructs).

## 7. Architecture Impact

Potential additive Shared Capability / Terminal assessment surfaces later. No architecture change authorized now.

## 8. Kernel Impact

Possible richer Enterprise descriptive metadata later; must not collapse Permission into discovery scores.

## 9. Runtime Impact

Assisted discovery via AI Runtime only under approval/audit bounds; no unmanaged side effects.

## 10. Smart Terminal Impact

Guided discovery workspaces and readiness dashboards; Terminal is not enterprise truth source.

## 11. Enterprise Brain Impact

Dossiers fuel advisory insights/simulations; Brain must not auto-execute evolution.

## 12. Marketplace Impact

Later: assessment packages / industry playbooks with declared data scope.

## 13. Developer Platform Impact

Later: discovery SDKs, assessment package manifests, facilitator extension patterns—Package/SDK surfaces only after promotion.

## 14. Potential Blueprint Impact

Knowledge, AI, UI/Smart Terminal, Package blueprint candidates for discovery data shapes and UX.

## 15. Potential Constitutional Impact

Possible future BOOK02/BOOK04 (and readiness linkage to BOOK03) proposals—recommendation only.

## 16. Validation Requirements

## 17. Enterprise Pilot Strategy

Observational pilots (manufacturing + non-manufacturing); worksheets only in Wave 1; compare human-facilitated vs AI-assisted discovery quality.

## 18. Success Criteria

## 20. Migration Strategy

Enterprises migrate from ad-hoc assessments → versioned Discovery Dossiers → Evolution Engine inputs. No rip-and-replace of ERP/MES required for Wave 1. Migration is advisory process change first; platform migration only after promotion.

## 21. Long-term Evolution

Continuous discovery refresh cadence; industry overlays (RP-008); DNA deepening (RP-002); capability-first operating model (RP-003); permanent Research Asset even after productization of subsets.
